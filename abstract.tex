\begin{abstract}
    We revisit the semantics of the intuitionistic modal logic $\K$ and introduce a new semantic framework based on \emph{weaved models}. In brief, a weaved model is a collection of Kripke models linked by explicitly represented simulations, allowing intuitionistic and modal accessibility relations to be treated in a modular fashion. We show that this semantics integrates cleanly with standard tools from classical modal logic: unraveling, pruning, bisimulations, and $n$-bisimulations all admit natural counterparts in the weaved setting.
    We further demonstrate that filtration over subformula-closed sets behaves particularly well: it preserves simulations, and produces weaved models with Kripke models whose size is bounded by the filtration base. By combining all these tools, we obtain a simple proof of the finite model property for intuitionistic $\K$ with respect to weaved semantics. Altogether, these results position weaved models as a modular and technically robust semantic foundation for the model theory of intuitionistic modalities.
\end{abstract}
